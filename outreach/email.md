Three versions. All say the same thing at different lengths.

**Before you send anything, read the two flags at the bottom of this file.**
One is about a price you may need to re-check, one is about who you address it
to.

## Notes on the choices

**The opening line.** It comes from two places in the research, both real:

- `input/reviews.txt` — Tonya R.'s Google review thanks three staff members by
  first name. Fabiana C.'s says "the nicest, most professional, most caring
  people". Kyla G.'s says "absolutely love the staff". Every single recoverable
  review is about the people, not the equipment or the results.
- `input/facts.txt` §3 — their `/team/` page names ten people with full
  credentials, including Beth Barrus, RN, co-founder, "more than 50 years of
  nursing experience".

That combination is true, particular to them, and impossible to paste into
another company's email. It is also a compliment they have earned rather than
flattery, which is why it opens the message.

**The one thing that is wrong.** Their homepage has a "Google Reviews" heading
with nothing rendered beneath it (`input/facts.txt` §5). I chose this over the
bigger structural problem — the ~190 near-duplicate city pages — for three
reasons. It is visible to them in ten seconds, so it is checkable rather than
a claim. It costs them money directly, because it is an empty space exactly
where a nervous GLP-1 patient looks for proof. And it is a small, fixable,
non-humiliating thing to have pointed out, whereas opening a cold email by
telling someone their SEO vendor has built them a doorway-page problem is a
teardown. Save that one for a conversation. It is in `notes.md`.

**Why no mention of the price.** The email's job is to get the link opened. The
deck does the pricing. Putting $1,000 in a first cold email turns it into a
sales pitch, and the whole premise here is that it is not one yet.

**Why the last question is what it is.** "Would you rather I sent this to
someone else there?" is easy to answer in four words, costs them nothing, and
quietly solves the real routing problem: `info@` is a patient-enquiry inbox and
your message may be sitting with a receptionist who cannot act on it. It gives
them a way to help you without committing to anything.

## FLAG 1 — who to address it to

The email above is deliberately addressed to nobody by name, because it is going
to `info@`, which is a shared inbox.

**If you send the Instagram DM instead — which I recommend — do not open with
"Hi".** The likeliest person reading it is Samantha Nunley (Business Development
& Patient Experience Manager) or Kim Barrus (founder). Neither is confirmed as
the account operator, so I have not put a name in the copy. If you can tell from
their recent posts or story replies who runs it, use that name; it is worth more
than any other edit you could make to this message.

**Kim B. Barrus, Ph.D. is the decision-maker** — founder, Chief of Operations,
explicitly not the clinician. If you ever get a name-level route to him, take
it. See `outreach/contact.md`.

## FLAG 2 — the thing that could embarrass you

The concept site publishes **their real prices**, read from their inner pages on
12 August 2026: IV drips $149–$199, HydraFacial $150/$175/$210, weight
management $550/month.

**Re-confirm those before you send the link**, or at minimum before any call.
Prices on a med spa's site go stale, their HydraFacial page was running a
"50% off" promo popup when I gathered, and a wrong price on a page you built is
the fastest way to lose the credibility the whole approach depends on.

Checking takes two minutes: open their `/vitamin-iv-drips/`,
`/the-hydrafacial-in-agoura-hills/` and `/weight-management/` pages and compare.
